Title: The Patient Quant: Building a Machine-Learned Portfolio for One Investor, and Measuring What It Is Actually Worth

Creator: Gerald Enrique Nelson Mc Kenzie
Publisher: Tico Internet LLC
Edition: 2026 First Edition
License: Apache License 2.0
DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24527200

**This book builds one complete investment system for a single investor and runs it over 291 months of history, net of trading costs and of the capital gains tax, and reports what it is actually worth.** The universe is sixty-one equity and bond exchange-traded funds; the strategic allocation is a stated policy that the model is never allowed to set; the machine's job is to estimate the posterior over the edge and to size the position from it. The system earns 8.42 per cent a year with a Sharpe ratio of 0.796 and a worst drawdown of -33.40 per cent, against 10.85 per cent and -50.84 per cent for the index bought and held, and 7.47 per cent in the same portfolio held in a taxable account.

**The result is a null result, and the book reports it as one: the layer that forecasts returns and tilts the portfolio inside its sleeves contributes about -0.02 percentage points a year, and the layer that slows the portfolio down when volatility rises and brakes it when the drawdown deepens contributes about +0.21 percentage points a year and about -2.27 points of drawdown.** The protection is positive in every one of the 20 starting windows the book tests. The test that chose the system is reported too, and it is unflattering: the probability of backtest overfitting is 0.571, which is worse than a coin flip, and the average correlation between the members of the trial family is 0.99435, so the twenty-five members are worth about 1.14 independent trials rather than twenty-five. **Every number is produced by one generator and re-derived at build time, the Python package, the data cache, the source ledger and the verification gates are published with the book, and the arithmetic can therefore be checked, an assumption changed and the argument re-run.**
